🌎 Theoretical & Practical Geodesy in Multi-Hemisphere Atoll Environments

The Republic of Kiribati encompasses 33 islands dispersed across 3.44 million km² of ocean, straddling both the Equator and the 180° meridian. This creates "The Three-Zone Problem"—spatial fragmentation across the Northern and Southern Hemispheres, Eastern/Western Longitudes, and non-contiguous UTM zones. The MFOR-GSD Spatial Reference Rule enforces master unprojected storage in WGS 84 (EPSG:4326/4327) combined with on-demand projection into native UTM zones during metric analysis.

Day 1 rule (adopted): Store the master copy unprojected in WGS 84; project on demand into the native UTM zone for every metric step; record both the storage CRS and the analysis CRS against every dataset. A coordinate frame mixed between global, national-grid and local systems produces a displacement, not noise.

✗ STRICTLY PROHIBITED Unadjusted EGM2008 Conversions

The 400mm EGM2008 Gravimetric Tilt Artifact ⚠️ re-verify source

Global geopotential models (EGM2008) exhibit an unmodeled ~400 mm (0.40 m) gravimetric tilt across Tarawa. Because natural terrain elevation rarely exceeds 3.0 m, applying EGM2008 introduces a 13.3% vertical full-scale error, artificially submerging whole villages on one end while elevating vulnerable causeways on the other.

Physical Cause: Steep basaltic volcanic seamounts (ρ ≈ 2.9 g/cm³) rising 4,000m from abyssal ocean water (ρ ≈ 1.03 g/cm³) create extreme gravity gradients truncated at degree/order 2160 in satellite altimetry.

📏 National Vertical Reference Frame (Betio TGZ) & Outer-Island TBM SOP

All coastal topographic and bathymetric elevations are standardized to Betio Tide Gauge Zero (TGZ), anchored by Primary Benchmark KIR 1 (+3.5334 m TGZ) ⚠️ provisional. Direct ellipsoidal-to-orthometric raster conversions utilize a single scalar vertical translation parameter ΔZ = ZKIR1, TGZ - zKIR1, Ellipsoid to preserve raw RTK/PPK surface slope free of global geoid tilt.

Datum-consistency rule (Day 3): before any inundation comparison, confirm the vertical reference of the elevation surface and of the water level, and convert one if they differ. On a Pacific atoll these differences reach several decimetres — the same order as the change being mapped.

🜁 Drone Survey SOP — LiDAR & RTK/PPK Photogrammetry

Airborne data collection uses two routes: the DJI Matrice 300 RTK with the Zenmuse L1 payload (Livox Avia LiDAR + 20MP RGB) and D-RTK 2 base, or an RTK/PPK camera drone class (e.g. P4 RTK / Mavic 3E) for photogrammetry. The method, flight parameters and base-station discipline are chosen per mission from the decision tree below. Both routes target ASPRS 5-cm-class vertical accuracy when controlled correctly.

🧮 Choose Your Method — LiDAR vs RTK/PPK Photogrammetry Decision rule from training Day 2

GSD ≈ one-third of the target accuracy is the working margin: a 3 cm requirement therefore needs ~1 cm GSD. Choose the sensor by canopy, deliverable, accuracy and cost.

✓ Use LiDAR (M300 + L1)
  • Closed or dense canopy (mangrove, babai, coconut) — photogrammetry cannot see the ground.
  • Bare-earth DTM under vegetation is the required deliverable (CSF-classified).
  • Triple-return LiDAR + RGB in one pass; independent of texture matching.
  • Higher cost/throughput; needs D-RTK 2 base on a surveyed benchmark.
✓ Use Photogrammetry (P4 RTK / Mavic 3E class)
  • Open terrain, shoreline, built edge — the coast and village fabric.
  • Orthomosaic planimetry + DSM/DTM from image matching; 1 cm-class GSD achievable.
  • Lower cost and faster per km²; works on RTK or PPK (see below).
  • Avoid over repetitive canopy/low texture (needs higher overlap, 80/75).

Autonomous single-point base station averaging is STRICTLY PROHIBITED. In equatorial ionospheric environments, single-point averaging introduces 1.50m to 3.00m vertical shifts. Field operators MUST execute manual coordinate entry in DJI Pilot 2 before arming aircraft motors:

1. Plumb over benchmark KIR 1 or certified MFOR-TBM.
2. Verify locking tripod APC height offset = 1.8015 m.
3. Enter certified benchmark lat/long to 9 decimal places.

📷 Camera Discipline & Aircraft Settings (pass/fail gate)

  • ✓ Mechanical shutter mandatory — electronic (rolling) shutter leans straight edges on a moving aircraft.
  • ✓ Minimum shutter 1/1000 s; at 12 m/s that moves the aircraft 12 mm (≈1 pixel at 1 cm GSD). Set shutter first — never auto-exposure overcast.
  • ✓ TimeSync active before mobilising; after the flight the event-mark count must equal the image count, one entry per image in order.
  • ✓ Smart Oblique Capture for coast and built edges — oblique gimbal shots at line ends add façade/reef data with ~20-50% less flight time than dedicated obliques.
  • ✓ Fly beyond the AOI on every side — model quality falls off at the mosaic edge; include the coastal margin and the control ground.
Weather go / delay / stand-down is decided against written limits before mobilisation — wind at operating height, cloud (no auto-exposure), and salt/rain on the lens. Record the decision either way.

Ground Target Deployment per 1.5km Block

Ground Control Points (GCPs) 3 - 5 Targets

60x60cm high-contrast matte checkerboards in double-chain staggered "sawtooth" perimeter layout. Used exclusively for Bundle Block Adjustment calibration — spread across the site, never clustered at one end.

Independent Checkpoints (CPs) 5 - 10 Targets

Strictly withheld from processing. Stratified across 4 atoll geomorphological zones for independent QA/QC verification:

  • • Stratum 1 (Reef Flat/Beachrock): 2-3 CPs (NVA)
  • • Stratum 2 (Beach Scarp/Seawall): 2-3 CPs (NVA)
  • • Stratum 3 (Berm Crest/Road): 1-2 CPs (NVA)
  • • Stratum 4 (Vegetation/Mangrove): 1-2 CPs (VVA)
Accuracy is not RMSE alone: checkpoints detect scatter well and systematic tilt badly. A model can pass its RMSE and still carry a real vertical bias — control spread independently across the site is what proves position.

💻 Data Processing Pipeline, Hydro-Conditioning & ASPRS Accuracy Certification

Raw point clouds (LiDAR) or image sets (photogrammetry) are aligned/registered, bare-earth extracted (CSF), hydro-conditioned, and certified against ASPRS 2023 Edition 2 Standards using independent checkpoints. For photogrammetry the chain is images → alignment → sparse cloud → dense cloud → mesh → orthomosaic + DEM; each stage is checked because a defect introduced at one stage first becomes visible two stages later.

🧵 Cloth Simulation Filter (CSF) Mandatory Parameters

The 3D point cloud is inverted upside down; a simulated rigid cloth is dropped under gravity to filter dense coconut palms and mangrove roots while preserving carbonate terrain micro-relief.

💧 Depression Breaching vs. Taro Pit Preservation

⚠️ PROHIBITION: Standard "Fill Depressions" Banned

Global depression filling erases authentic babai (giant swamp taro) pits and borrow pits, destroying real flood storage capacity. Analysts must use Least-Cost Depression Breaching in WhiteboxTools (max_dist = 25.0m) to cut only artificial causeway road blockages.

Marine Hydro-Flattening:

Water-edge breakline polygons are digitized at low tide. Oceanward and lagoonward raster cells seaward of the breakline are burned to instantaneous water height (+0.500m TGZ) or NoData to guarantee flat hydrodynamic boundary conditions.

🛡 Spatial Data Governance, OGC Standards & Four-Tier Commercial Licensing

MFOR-GSD mandates the formal deprecation of ESRI Shapefiles in favor of OGC GeoPackage (.gpkg) and Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF (.tif/COG). All products are governed by CARE Indigenous Data Sovereignty principles and metadata harvested via ISO 19115-1 XML to the Kiribati National Repository and SPC Pacific Data Hub.

Format Mandate: Legacy Shapefile Deprecation .SHP PROHIBITED

✗ Legacy Shapefile Vulnerabilities (.shp)
  • 10-character attribute header truncation mangles field names (crest_elev_tgz_m → crest_el_1).
  • Multi-file sidecar fragmentation causes lost projection (.prj) files during island transfers.
  • Strict 2 GB file size ceiling prevents storing atoll-wide building or shoreline vector tables.
✓ Mandated OGC GeoPackage Standard (.gpkg)
  • Single, self-contained SQLite3 database container supporting up to 140 TB.
  • Full UTF-8 character encoding preserves native Gilbertese place names.
  • Embedded spatial indexing (R*Tree), vector layer styles, and ISO metadata tables.
File Geodatabase is a legitimate local container only where receiving-agency Esri licensing requires it — never the default, and always delivered with a note saying why.

🗃 Catalogue Entry (National Repository pattern) ISO 19115-1 metadata

Every layer entered exactly as the national catalogue columns require:

  • • Thematic type — from the controlled vocabulary (Hazards, Surveyed, Topography, Buildings, Population, Boundaries, ...).
  • • GeoPackage database — named to repository convention, one file, many layers.
  • • Layer name(s) — pattern island_purpose_custodian_tier, e.g. ABA_TOPO_Buildings_LMD_TRNSF.
  • • Summary — names island, feature, date AND source (the four things a searcher filters on).
  • • Dataset date — when the data represents, NOT the file date (a shoreline digitized 2024 from 2011 imagery has dataset date 2011).
  • • Custodian — a named person with a working contact, not a division in general.
  • • Status (tier) + Superceded date — populated only when replaced.
Coastal hazard layers carry 5 domain fields: hazard_type (erosion|inundation|combined) • horizon_year (2040|2060|2080) • method (dsas_rate|lidar_screening|composite) • confidence (high|moderate|low) • source_file (relative path). Version numbers live in metadata/catalogue — never as a filename suffix.

Start restricted where data carries personal information or community/landowner agreements — easier to release than to recall. Record the tier in the layer name and the catalogue Status: recorded twice is deliberate, remembered-zero is how data leaks. 🌊 Coastal & Climate Hydrology — Survey-to-Analysis Pipeline

MFOR-GSD drone products

Survey Product LiDAR point cloud (m TGZ) / RTK-photogrammetry DSM → CSF-classified ground + off-ground
Hydro-conditioning Subtract water mask → fill pits → breach causeways/roads (least-cost, ≤25 m) → keep babai pits
Flood-ready DEM Bare-earth DEM clipped to island, no NoData holes, +0.5 m TGZ marine reference for hydro-flattening
Hydrological Uses Bathtub inundation screening, storm-surge scenarios, drainage & flood routing, SLR planning layers
⚠️ DSM ≠ DTM. A photogrammetric DSM carries trees, houses and vehicles; a LiDAR DTM is the bare ground. Inundation and drainage analysis must run on the bare-earth DEM — using the DSM adds the height of whatever sits on the ground to your flood surface and makes roads flood-safe that are not. Never run bathtub screening on a DSM.

Step 2 — Bathtub fill + sea connectivity

Classify every cell below the threshold as flooded, then apply the connectivity filter (CONN_MARGIN = 0.15 m): a cell is flooded only if it connects to open water at or below threshold+0.15. Keeps rim-impoundment basins separate from true ocean flooding.

Step 3 — Road / causeway breaching

Fill only impounds water behind causeways and raised roads. Breach with least-cost path, max_dist = 25 m, cutting asphalt at its lowest local pass. Preserve babai (swamp-taro) pits — they are perennially wet land, not floodable dry land; a filled babai pit silently raises the water table estimate.

Step 4 — NoData trap (the −3.4e38 defect)

Undeclared NoData at −3.4e38 acts as a giant pit that bathtub fill floods completely, producing a false inland sea. Before any analysis, ask two questions about every DEM: (1) does it declare nodata and match the actual hole value? (2) area-weighted mean elevation > −10 m (a sane sanity check for an atoll)?

Screening ≠ modelling

Bathtub screening marks land that a given water level could reach. It does not model timing, surge hydrographs, wave run-up or flow through channels. For engineered flood statements use a hydrodynamic model (HEC-RAS / Delft3D-FM) — and note Kiribati has almost no nearshore bathymetry, so model inputs must be planned as a separate survey campaign.

Rim-impoundment reality

In the IKO-flood runs the island rim acts as a bowl: 321.84 ha of land below T3 with only a 5.7 cm lip to keep the ocean out — breaching the rim drops interior flooding by 86%. This is why the connectivity filter and breaching steps are not optional polish; they are the difference between an alarm and an evacuation plan.

🔧 Atoll Hydro-conditioning of the DEM

  • 1. Marine hydro-flatten: set open-water, lagoon and ocean cells to a reference of +0.5 m TGZ so the DEM is not flooded by its own noise and coastal gradients are real.
  • 2. Pit filling: fill single-cell depressions that are artefacts. Do not blanket-fill — it erases real micro-relief the hydrology depends on.
  • 3. Breaching: causeways, the South Tarawa road artery and runway lips only — least-cost, ≤25 m, lowest pass.
  • 4. Protect babai pits (see Step 3 opposite).
  • 5. Keep micro-relief: CSF at res 1.0 m · rigid 1 · thresh 0.15 m keeps the decimeters that decide where a puddle becomes a pond.
  • 6. Output layers: DEM (conditioned), flow-direction, flow-accumulation, and a catchment/pond layer — each with the catalogue tier and date you just finished assigning.
For lagoonal flats and reclaimed land, a differential survey (±5 mm class, levelling or PPK base) beats DEM interpolation every time — reclaimed land is the places Kiribati will build on next.

🧮 Software Recipes — free, runnable today

QGIS + GDAL — NoData mask & void fill: build a mask from gdal_calc.py ((B==-3.4e38)|(B<=-3.4e38)), interpolate voids, re-declare nodata. Condition with built-in Fill sinks, then Channel network.
